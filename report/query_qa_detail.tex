The recurring weaknesses are Q3 (17 partial hardware/software profiles), Q7 (14 partial and two absent measurement-control descriptions), Q5 (15 partial comparator assessments), and Q9 (12 partial validity discussions). All 21 score adequately on Q1, Q2, Q4 and Q6; this says the questions, mechanisms, workloads and metric labels were identifiable, not that results across papers use a common GPU, load, percentile, or fair baseline. Only two item scores are zero, both on Q7. A high sum can therefore coexist with a serious missing control in a specific comparison.

\paragraph{Sensitivity and version control.} Removing the four moderate reports leaves 17 high-scored studies; it does not erase an entire mechanism family, but any claim relying chiefly on one moderate report must be qualified. The preferred Reef journal extension scores 8.5; its earlier OSDI report scored 8 and is retained as version history, not a second independent study. A metadata hit or retrieval lead receives no QA score.

\paragraph{Limits of the appraisal.} Some supplied conversions lacked readable chart images; Q8 is based on accessible captions, tables and stated numbers, not reverse-engineered pixels. The experiments and code were not rerun, so Q10 assesses whether a reader can critically interpret the configuration. Performance isolation, resource separation, adversarial security and fault containment need different experiments; a high total cannot establish all four. \pending{Pantheon and Jormungandr remain unscored pending report-version and eligibility sign-off; if admitted, their own source-anchored Q1--Q10 rows and all summary counts must be regenerated.}
