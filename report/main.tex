\section{Research objective and engineering problem}
An engineer must share a single GPU among concurrent inference tenants. More multiplexing can improve utilization and tenant density, while potentially worsening interference and tail latency. Stronger partitioning can improve predictability and resource boundaries but can introduce fragmentation, hardware restrictions, and operational cost. This review will systematically identify, appraise, and synthesize peer-reviewed primary evidence on time-slicing, NVIDIA Multi-Process Service (MPS), Multi-Instance GPU (MIG), and CUDA/API remoting, proxying, or virtualization for single-node multi-tenant inference. Its outcome is a conditional engineering recommendation, rather than a universal winner.

The scope is the single-node OS/runtime layer. Training-only and cluster-orchestration-only studies are outside scope unless they directly evaluate an eligible local sharing mechanism for inference.

\section{Research questions}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textbf{RQ1.}] Which single-node time-slicing, MPS, MIG, and API-remoting/virtualization mechanisms have been evaluated for multi-tenant inference, and what isolation boundaries do they provide?
\item[\textbf{RQ2.}] How do studies measure utilization, throughput, tenant density, latency distribution/tail latency, interference, and fault isolation, and how comparable are their experiments?
\item[\textbf{RQ3.}] Under what workload, GPU and partition configuration, batching and concurrency, and tenant-mix conditions do mechanisms change utilization and latency predictability?
\item[\textbf{RQ4.}] What trade-offs, implementation costs, limitations, and apparently conflicting findings are reported, and which experimental differences plausibly explain them?
\item[\textbf{RQ5.}] Which mechanism or combination should an engineer choose under specified inference-serving conditions, given evidence quality and uncertainty?
\end{enumerate}

\section{Eligibility criteria}
Include peer-reviewed English journal/conference primary studies that technically evaluate or compare an in-scope single-node GPU-sharing mechanism for multi-tenant inference and report sufficient full-text engineering evidence. Directly transferable inference evidence requires an explicit mechanism and transfer rationale. Exclude duplicate or less-complete reports; training-only work; cluster-only work; reviews, vendor documentation, blogs, editorials, posters, patents, slides, and abstracts; inaccessible full texts; and reports lacking relevant technical or evaluation detail. Surveys and vendor documentation may provide context but never count toward the target of 15--20 primary studies.

\section{Screening and PRISMA 2020}
All records will retain source provenance. DOI, normalized title, year, and author data will be used to link records, reports, and unique underlying studies without deleting records. Two reviewers will independently conduct title/abstract and full-text screening; a third reviewer will adjudicate disagreements. The actual PRISMA 2020 flow will be calculated from the logs: records identified, records removed before screening, records screened, reports sought, reports not retrieved (with documented access attempts), reports assessed, reports excluded with reasons, and unique studies included. Unretrieved reports will not be falsely placed among assessed-report exclusions. PRISMA 2020 provides the reporting checklist and suitable flow-diagram templates \cite{prisma}.

\section{Quality assessment}
Two reviewers score each included study from 0 to 1 on ten questions: clarity of objective; sharing configuration; hardware/software description; workload/tenant description; fairness of baseline; metric definition; measurement method; result support; limitations; and reproducibility. Scores are 1 (adequate), 0.5 (partial/unclear), or 0 (not reported). Total quality is High (8--10), Moderate (6--7.5), or Low ($<6$). Low-quality studies remain labelled eligible evidence but are excluded in a sensitivity synthesis.

\section{Data extraction and evidence synthesis}
For each report, capture identity and access state; mechanism/configuration; GPU/SKU and partition; OS/kernel, driver and CUDA/runtime; model/workload; batching/arrival pattern; tenant/co-tenant mix; baseline; metric definitions and units; exact results with page/figure/table anchors; isolation dimensions; limitations; and quality score. Isolation is recorded separately as performance interference, resource/memory partitioning, security boundary, and fault containment, each as measured, claimed, not reported, or not applicable.

Evidence will be synthesized by mechanism category: temporal sharing, MPS, MIG, remoting/virtualization, and hybrids. Quantitative values will be compared only in compatible experimental strata; heterogeneous results receive structured narrative synthesis with stated conditions and evidence strength. A sensitivity synthesis will omit low-quality or incomplete evidence. The final decision matrix will condition recommendations on latency SLO, utilization and tenant density, isolation need, MIG hardware availability, workload heterogeneity, and operational complexity.
